Asking ChatGPT to pick a $20 put

Nov 27, 2026

Preface

I was trying to understand options — just a little bit, just to see what's up. I've never bought an option, ever. I did enable it on my Robinhood, but I've never bought one. I'm really not that into trading; I just buy ETFs, pretty much. But I figured I'd try to learn through doing it: buy some sort of option for $20 and see what happens.

I didn't want to spend a few days finding the perfect one. Just a halfway decent one, I'll just get it, and whatever. Kind of like buying a lottery ticket.

So I opened ChatGPT, sent it screenshots of my Robinhood option chain, and asked it to walk me through a decent middle-of-the-road put. Twenty-two messages later, I hadn't bought anything.

Chat

The conversation started on the S&P 500 index itself, SPX, around 7,297. ChatGPT's framework was sensible enough: a moderate-risk put, 30–45 days out, strike near the money — a 7,300 strike at a $30 premium, $3,000 a contract, breakeven 7,270. The problem was that the put I had actually selected on my screen was a December 28 SPX 9,400 put, which would have cost about $208,451. ChatGPT talked me off it, correctly.

Then came the budget: $20, tell me exactly what put to buy. That's when the S&P 500 got too expensive for me in every form. SPX options are $100 a point, so ChatGPT moved me to SPY, the ETF — same index, smaller bets. But SPY was around $728, and its December 18 puts cost $1,365 to $1,614 a contract. The only ones that fit $20 were the far-out ones: a $495 put for $20.04, which would only pay off if SPY fell 32% in 21 days, to below $494.80. A January 15 put was worse — a $365 strike, betting on a roughly 50% fall in 49 days.

So we went smaller: SPYM, a cheaper S&P 500 ETF at about $85.72. Its December 4 $81 put fit the budget at $20.04 — but it needed a 5.7% fall in seven days, which ChatGPT called fairly aggressive, not middle-of-the-road. We detoured into VIXW twice by accident (a volatility product, the opposite of what I wanted), then to IWM, the Russell 2000 ETF at $274.10. Its December 18 $210 put cost $20.04 and needed a 23.5% fall in 21 days. ChatGPT's verdict on that chain: pass.

By the end it was honest about it: no qualifying contract identified, and it didn't want to give me a bad trade just to fill the $20 budget. It asked whether I'd raise the budget to $40–$50.

One detail from the screenshots worth noting: my Robinhood screen showed $0 buying power available, the whole way through. I couldn't have bought any of these anyway.

Notes

  1. ChatGPT's rejections were, on the math, right. Every $20 contract it found was a lottery ticket — a 32% drop, a 50% drop, a 23.5% drop. Saying no to those was correct.
  2. But the experience still felt incoherent. The assistant kept saying "let's find one" and then, at each step, "don't buy this." Twenty-two messages of being guided toward a trade and then talked out of it.
  3. The $20 constraint made the task nearly impossible from the start, and ChatGPT never said so upfront. It discovered that fact one screenshot at a time, with me doing the legwork.
  4. We disagreed about what the $20 was for. I was treating it as tuition — money I was fine throwing out the window to learn something. ChatGPT treated it as money to protect.
  5. It's still not easy to tell which model you're talking to. The shared page shows only ChatGPT branding and a title — "Choisir un put modéré," in French, for a conversation conducted entirely in English.

Reflection

This is a different failure from the ones in the earlier posts. Nothing was refused, and nothing was hallucinated. The advice was mostly right, and I still walked away with nothing — not even the bad trade I asked for. The assistant optimized for not being wrong rather than for the thing I actually asked it to do, which was: pick one, I'll buy it, and I'll learn from the outcome either way.

It also felt like there's an arbitrage opportunity in here somewhere: at this point, people still need to have trading knowledge, at least if you're doing it by hand. Maybe that's not even true if people are doing high-frequency trading — that's just not stuff I know about. But ChatGPT, honestly, did not seem like a great trading assistant.

Conclusion

I didn't buy anything. The $20 put stayed a hypothetical, and the lesson stayed hypothetical too. That's my experience using ChatGPT today: not wrong in a way you can point at, just not coherent enough to actually do the thing it was helping me do.
