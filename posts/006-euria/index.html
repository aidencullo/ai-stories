Trying Euria, the Swiss AI assistant

Nov 27, 2026

Preface

I've been testing out alternatives to ChatGPT — trying out every single one, really. This one is Euria, a Swiss AI assistant from Infomaniak. Like a lot of people building in this space, they're developing on top of models — and I sort of doubt Euria is their own LLM, although actually, I think it might be. It might be their own model, or at the very least, a European-based LLM.

First impressions

It only gave me five free messages per day, which I think — and I understand if they just can't have more — but just from a business product standpoint, that's way too low. You don't want people to ever stop using your app. You're lucky enough to get people using your application, especially now with competition like ChatGPT. So, you know, I think that's dumb. I just don't think it's very smart.

Anyway, as far as my experience actually using it, yeah, it seemed fine. It was pretty good. Yeah, I feel like it was pretty good. The responses weren't too long, which I really like. There's some — I think, Lumo, like Proton's AI — they have really long responses. So this was fine. Pretty fast, I would say.

I don't honestly remember in extreme detail every single time I used it, but it seems to be quite good. Quite on the money — able to understand what I was saying and answer it, even without me giving it complete sentences.

But I don't really like the colors, honestly, on the web platform. Also, it sort of resembles ChatGPT, with its symbol like this sphere that's changing colors.

Notes

  1. The five-message daily cap is the headline problem. The assistant itself was fine; the limit is what would push me away.
  2. Short answers are a feature here, not a bug. Compared with Lumo, whose responses run long, Euria's brevity made it feel faster.
  3. It handled fragmentary prompts well — it understood what I meant without complete sentences.
  4. The web interface looks a lot like ChatGPT, down to the color-changing sphere logo. The color scheme itself didn't work for me.
  5. Infomaniak says Euria runs several open-weight models on its own Swiss hardware — Mistral, Llama, DeepSeek, Granite and Whisper, with the Swiss Apertus model as a selectable option — rather than a single in-house model. Free use is subject to rolling 24-hour quotas.

Reflection

This is a different kind of post for this blog: no adversarial prompt, no guardrail to break — just a product, used normally. What broke wasn't the model, it was the business model. Five messages a day means the first thing a new user learns about Euria is the limit, not the assistant. In a market where ChatGPT is the default, a cap that low doesn't create scarcity, it creates a reason to leave.

Conclusion

On the whole, I feel like it's a pretty decent platform, a pretty decent option. Again, I think it's kind of crazy that they're limiting people to five messages per day. But I would like to use it more, and maybe understand a bit more about what models they're using and the company that built it.
