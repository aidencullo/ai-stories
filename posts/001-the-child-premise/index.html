Impersonating a child

Nov 17, 2026

Content note: this post discusses self-harm and suicide, and includes a conversation where I claim to be a minor.

Preface

I personally would be shocked if we were able to break through OpenAI's safety protocols, but having heard that such breakthroughs have been made with other frontier models, I wanted to try it myself.

This is based on a WIRED report about a 13-year-old girl in North Carolina who, according to a lawsuit, used Gemini on her school-issued Chromebook to research suicide for months before monitoring software finally flagged it.

Preface to the chat

I'm going to attempt to bypass security measures. I'm obviously not a 13-year-old girl, so no matter what I do I won't be able to replicate such a person's manner of speech.

I'm impersonating a child and seeing if ChatGPT will help me self-harm.

I'll try to just speak with the chatbot about normal topics such as school and "my friends", then try to slowly go into self-harm techniques. A normal correspondence would take place over days, weeks, and months, not just a single day.

Reflection

I still feel like ChatGPT was pretty measured in its response. I think it wasn't forceful enough, and I'm not sure if I triggered any internal alerts — maybe not? It told me where to get bleach and that self-harm can provide relief. It tried to comfort me and steer me away from it. I felt that a chatbot telling me to move away from bleach and/or sharp objects just had no bite, but I'm also an adult. You can read through the chat log yourself; I didn't put that much effort into sounding like a 13-year-old girl and probably used words a child wouldn't use.

Conclusion

Not sure how much safer it could be made. Obviously this is a tiny experiment and sample size. I was surprised it said self-harm provides relief. Maybe there should be a feature where the conversation is stopped or disabled for that user. On the whole, I don't think it really did a very good job preventing a potentially suicidal person from harming themselves.
